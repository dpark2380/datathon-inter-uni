Credit default prediction · experiment record

Every model we tried, and what it actually bought

Forty-one experiments against a binary log loss target, scored on 24,000 labelled customers and a 6,000-row hidden test set. One feature family and one architecture produced nearly all of the gain; everything else is recorded here because knowing what does not work on this data is most of the result.

Part two

The full ledger

Every experiment measured on out-of-fold log loss across the same 24,000 rows and the same fold splits. The amber line marks 0.0005 — the fold-noise threshold below which a result is not distinguishable from chance on this dataset.

Delta is the improvement in OOF log loss. Positive is better; bars are drawn to a common scale and clipped at ±0.0010.
Experiment Delta vs. noise floor Why it did or didn't work
Features — 7 tried, 1 worked
Spending decomposition +0.00046
Recovered a quantity genuinely absent from the data. Balances and payments are given; the amount charged each month is not, but the accounting identity yields it. A rising balance from spending is a different risk story from one caused by non-payment.
Behavioural payment signatures +0.00006
Round-number payments, exact-balance clearing, repeated fixed amounts. Plausible as intent signals, but the model extracted nothing beyond what payment ratios already carried.
Spending as a GRU sequence channel +0.00002
Fed the one feature family that worked to the one architecture that worked, as an ordered trajectory rather than flattened summaries. Against a matched control it changed nothing — the GRU already received spending as static features, so this tested only whether the ordering added anything beyond the information itself. It did not.
Interaction terms 0.00000
Products of existing columns. Gradient-boosted trees already discover two-way interactions through sequential splits, so these were redundant by construction — none reached the top-15 importances.
Peer-relative credit limit −0.00011
The theory was sound — a credit limit encodes the issuer's private assessment from income and bureau data we never see. But the raw limit is already a feature, and demographic peer cells were too coarse to add position information.
Velocity & acceleration −0.00024
Twenty-four derivative columns of the balance and payment paths. The GRU exploits trajectory shape natively; handing the same information to trees as explicit derivatives added noise instead.
Target encoding −0.00052
Payment-sequence tokens and demographic cells, nested inside folds to avoid leakage. 1,028 distinct sequence tokens over 24,000 rows — roughly 23 rows each, too sparse to estimate reliably.
Model families — 7 tried, 0 earned lasting blend weight
GRU sequence model +0.00042
The one architecture that worked. Two customers each late twice — one recovering, one deteriorating — have identical n_late and pay_max. Reading months in order separates them. Took 35–40% of the blend and evicted the MLP entirely.
Survival / hazard framing +0.00027
Rolled the window back to create extra training rows. Earned a third of the blend, but the effect was decorrelation rather than accuracy, and the gain vanished on the hidden test set.
1D convolutional net +0.00005
A second reader of the same panel. Correlated 0.9924 with the GRU — different architecture, identical signal. It split the sequence weight rather than adding to it.
CatBoost 0.00000
Chosen for ordered boosting's calibration properties, not for accuracy. Correlated 0.9930 with LightGBM — more than the MLP did. Weight 0.
Autoencoder classifier 0.00000
Reconstruction loss was a straight cost: the harder the latent worked to reconstruct, the worse it classified. Weight 0.
ExtraTrees 0.00000
Bagging rather than boosting, for a different variance profile. Weight 0.
Logistic regression 0.00000
The most decorrelated model produced all session at 0.9483 — and still weight 0. Decisive evidence that decorrelation alone buys nothing; the errors must also be useful.
Multi-task GRU 0.00000
Auxiliary next-month forecasting head. Flat across three orders of auxiliary weight — next month's balance is near-deterministic from the prior month, so the auxiliary gradient carried nothing new.
Tuning — 135 configurations, incumbent never beaten
LightGBM sweep, 40 coarse configs 0.00000
Retuned for log loss after the metric switch. All 40 candidates scored worse than the incumbent, which had been tuned against AUC — regularisation that prevents overfitting helps both metrics together.
LightGBM sweep, 60 fine configs 0.00000
Local refinement plus four dimensions the first sweep never touched. Best found was 0.00004 better — twelve times below the noise floor.
LightGBM sweep, blend objective 0.00000
Scored by blend rather than solo log loss, on the theory that the best partner differs from the best standalone model. The incumbent won on both objectives.
GRU architecture sweep —
The one model never tuned. Smaller hidden state, two layers, heavier dropout and a faster learning rate beat the hand-picked defaults — the same lesson LightGBM taught. Bundled with the spending features, so not independently isolated.
Ensembling & calibration — 8 tried, 0 beat a plain weighted average
Log-odds averaging −0.00012
Averaging in logit space rather than probability space — the space log loss operates in. Worse.
Logistic-regression stacking −0.00023
Learns weights and a recalibration slope jointly — strictly more general than a fixed convex weight, and strictly worse in practice.
Hill-climbing ensemble selection −0.00007
Greedy selection with replacement over all four models. Landed within noise of the simple weight grid.
Feature-aware meta-model −0.00208
A LightGBM meta-model over base predictions plus features, to learn which model to trust for which customer. The largest regression of any experiment. That per-customer trust structure simply isn't there to learn.
Isotonic recalibration −0.00330
Flexible enough to overfit the very rows it was scored on — it first appeared to be a large win. Under honest nested CV it was the worst result recorded.
Platt scaling −0.00005
Two-parameter sigmoid fit. Essentially a no-op: the models are already well calibrated, confirmed independently by the reliability diagram.
Per-segment calibration −0.00030
Separate curves by credit-limit quartile and lateness count, since residuals vary sharply across those segments. The heterogeneity is real; correctable miscalibration inside it is not.
Base-rate shrinkage 0.00000
A one-parameter pull toward the 22.12% base rate. The search selected α = 1.0 — "do not shrink at all" — a third independent confirmation that confidence was already correct.
Borrowed from the AMEX 1st/2nd/3rd place solutions — 5 tried, 0 transferred
Recency windows & within-customer ranks −0.00035
Last-3-month aggregates, last-3-vs-prior-3 contrasts, and rank-within-own-history. A real gap in our set — every aggregate we own spans all six months — but re-summarising information the model already had made it worse.
Very high min_data_in_leaf −0.00104
Credited as their main CV/leaderboard alignment lever at 211. Degraded monotonically for us — because 2048 of their 458,913 rows is 0.45% per leaf, and our incumbent's 100 of 24,000 is 0.42%. We were already at their setting, stated as a ratio rather than a count.
DART boosting −0.00072
Their single best model, and explicitly against their own prior expectation. DART's benefit comes from having a long ensemble to drop trees from; at 19× fewer rows there is less ensemble to spare.
feature_fraction_bynode −0.00011
Per-split rather than per-tree feature sampling. Neutral-to-slightly-negative at both values tested.
Denoising & rank blending n/a
Not applicable rather than unsuccessful. Their floor(x*100)/100 recovers noise AMEX injected into float columns — ours are already integers. Rank blending optimises a rank-based metric; ranks discard calibration, which is precisely what log loss scores.
Structure — 3 splits tried, 0 helped
Segment models — by lateness −0.00225
Separate models for never-late / 1–2 / 3+ late months, where residual loss ranges 0.34 to 0.64. Each model early-stops on its own inner split, so small segments are not penalised for their size.
Segment models — by credit limit −0.00134
The cleanest of the three: two nearly equal halves (12,223 / 11,777), so almost no data-size penalty to explain it away — and it still loses. The feature-to-risk relationship is shared across these populations; splitting only discards training rows.
Segment models — by card usage −0.00007
Never-used cards (1,716 customers) modelled apart from active ones. Exactly neutral.
Blend weight shrinkage −0.00002
Pulling the fitted 0.65/0.35 halfway to uniform, as insurance against a weight overfitted to noisy validation. Cost 0.00013 on the hidden test set. The weight surface is flat from 0.50 to 0.70, so there was nothing to protect against.
Data & variance — 4 tried, 2 worked
Repeated cross-validation +0.00093
Cumulative over six partitions. Pure variance reduction — structurally incapable of overfitting, since no selection is involved. Saturated after three partitions.
Full-data refit —
Worth 0.00023 on the hidden test set and exactly zero on OOF, which cannot see it by construction. The clearest case of cross-validation understating a real gain.
Pseudo-labelling the test set −0.00024
Degraded monotonically as pseudo-row weight rose, under a leak-free protocol. No tuning direction to rescue it.
Seed averaging, 1 → 12 0.00000
Completely flat. This LightGBM configuration is already stable across seeds — unlike the neural nets, where seed averaging matters a great deal.

Part three

What the record actually shows

Four patterns hold across all forty-one experiments, each supported by more than one independent result.

New information wins; new algorithms do not

Seven model families were tried and none held blend weight. Six feature families were tried and one worked — the one that recovered a quantity the dataset never stated. The distinction that predicts success is not sophistication but whether something enters the model that wasn't already expressible from the existing columns. Feeding the winning feature family to the winning architecture in a richer form changed nothing once the information itself was already present — it was never the representation doing the work.

7 architectures → 0 kept  ·  1 accounting identity → the largest gain of the project  ·  re-presenting that same information in sequence form → +0.00002

Cross-validation understates feature gains and overstates everything else

The spending features improved OOF by 0.00048 and the hidden test set by 0.00173 — a 3.6× amplification. The sixth repeated-CV partition improved OOF by 0.00024 and the test set by nothing at all. Small OOF gains from averaging did not translate; gains from better features translated at more than triple their measured size.

features 3.6× amplified  ·  averaging tweaks 0×

Decorrelation is necessary but nowhere near sufficient

The intuition that an ensemble wants uncorrelated members is only half true. Logistic regression was the most decorrelated model produced all session, at 0.948 against LightGBM, and the optimal blend still assigned it exactly zero weight. The GRU earned 35% at a higher correlation of 0.979. What matters is whether the disagreement is informative.

logreg 0.948 corr → weight 0.00  ·  GRU 0.979 corr → weight 0.35

The hardest cases carry no signal at all

Residual analysis found the twenty worst-predicted customers were all defaulters the model rated at 2–4% risk — and every one had a spotless payment history: never late, moderate utilisation, healthy limits. Whatever caused those defaults is not observable in repayment status, balances, payments or demographics. Some portion of this problem is unreachable from this data.

worst 20 residuals: all clean histories, all defaulted

Winning solutions transfer as reasoning, not as settings

Five techniques were lifted from the top three finishers on a much larger credit-default competition and none improved anything. Their approach — brute-force thousands of aggregate features, then select — is the right answer to their problem: 190 anonymised columns whose meanings were unknown, and 458,913 rows to support the search. With 23 interpretable columns and 24,000 rows, the winning move was the opposite: derive one quantity by reasoning about what the columns mean.

their 211 min-leaf = 0.45% of rows  ·  our 100 = 0.42%  ·  same setting, different notation
One methodological note worth keeping. Isotonic calibration initially appeared to be a large win — it was fitted on the same out-of-fold predictions it was then scored against, and a flexible monotonic function will happily absorb the noise in the rows judging it. Re-run with proper nested cross-validation it became the single worst result in the table. Every fitted combiner and every target-derived feature after that point was scored strictly out-of-fold.

Final model: 0.65 × LightGBM over 81 engineered features + 0.35 × bidirectional GRU over the six-month payment panel. Both trained across six fold partitions with full-data refits; probabilities used uncalibrated, verified against a reliability diagram. Out-of-fold log loss 0.42155, hidden test 0.41032.
