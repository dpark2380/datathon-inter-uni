Experiment results and model changes

This ledger records 41 experiments evaluated by out-of-fold binary log loss on 24,000 labelled customers. Nine configurations were also submitted to the hidden test set of 6,000 rows. Most of the improvement came from one feature family and one model architecture; the remaining results document which alternatives were tested and why they were not retained.

All experiments

All experiments were evaluated by out-of-fold log loss using the same 24,000 rows and fold splits. The amber line marks the 0.0005 noise threshold; differences below this value were not treated as reliable improvements.

Delta is the improvement in OOF log loss. Positive is better; bars are drawn to a common scale and clipped at ±0.0010.

Features — 7 tried, 1 worked
Spending decomposition+0.00046
The dataset provides balances and payments but not the amount charged each month. This value can be inferred from the accounting identity. It distinguishes a balance increase caused by new spending from one caused by reduced payments.
Behavioural payment signatures+0.00006
Round-number payments, exact-balance clearing, repeated fixed amounts. Plausible as intent signals, but the model extracted nothing beyond what payment ratios already carried.
Spending as a GRU sequence channel+0.00002
Spending was supplied to the GRU as an ordered trajectory rather than only as summary features. Since both variants already included static spending features, the test isolated the value of ordering. The 0.00002 change was within noise.
Interaction terms0.00000
Products of existing columns added no measurable value. Gradient-boosted trees can already represent two-way interactions through sequential splits, and none of the explicit interactions reached the top 15 features by importance.
Peer-relative credit limit−0.00011
A credit limit may encode the issuer’s private assessment from income and bureau data unavailable in this dataset. However, the raw limit was already a feature, and the demographic peer groups were too broad to add useful relative-position information.
Velocity & acceleration−0.00024
Twenty-four derivative columns of the balance and payment paths. The GRU exploits trajectory shape natively; handing the same information to trees as explicit derivatives added noise instead.
Target encoding−0.00052
Payment-sequence tokens and demographic cells, nested inside folds to avoid leakage. 1,028 distinct sequence tokens over 24,000 rows — roughly 23 rows each, too sparse to estimate reliably.
Model families — 10 tried, 3 kept, 7 challengers rejected
GRU sequence model+0.00042
The ordered sequence distinguishes customers with identical lateness aggregates but different trajectories, such as recovery versus recent deterioration. The GRU received 35–40% of the blend and replaced the MLP.
Survival / hazard framing+0.00027
Rolled the window back to create extra training rows. Earned a third of the blend, but the effect was decorrelation rather than accuracy, and the gain vanished on the hidden test set.
1D convolutional net+0.00005
The 1D-CNN processed the same monthly panel as the GRU and had a correlation of 0.9924 with its predictions. It divided the existing sequence-model weight without improving the ensemble.
CatBoost0.00000
Chosen for ordered boosting’s calibration properties, not for accuracy. Correlated 0.9930 with LightGBM — more than the MLP did. Weight 0.
Autoencoder classifier0.00000
Reconstruction loss was a straight cost: the harder the latent worked to reconstruct, the worse it classified. Weight 0.
ExtraTrees0.00000
Bagging rather than boosting, for a different variance profile. Weight 0.
Logistic regression0.00000
Logistic regression had the lowest correlation with LightGBM at 0.9483 but still received zero weight. Lower correlation alone did not improve the ensemble.
Multi-task GRU0.00000
Auxiliary next-month forecasting head. Flat across three orders of auxiliary weight — next month’s balance is near-deterministic from the prior month, so the auxiliary gradient carried nothing new.
Tuning — 135 configurations, incumbent never beaten
LightGBM sweep, 40 coarse configs0.00000
Retuned for log loss after the metric switch. All 40 candidates scored worse than the incumbent, which had been tuned against AUC — regularisation that prevents overfitting helps both metrics together.
LightGBM sweep, 60 fine configs0.00000
Local refinement plus four dimensions the first sweep never touched. Best found was 0.00004 better — twelve times below the noise floor.
LightGBM sweep, blend objective0.00000
Scored by blend rather than solo log loss, on the theory that the best partner differs from the best standalone model. The incumbent won on both objectives.
GRU architecture sweep—
The sweep selected a smaller hidden state, two layers, heavier dropout and a faster learning rate over the initial settings. Its individual effect cannot be measured because it was submitted with the spending features.
Ensembling & calibration — 8 tried, 0 beat a plain weighted average
Log-odds averaging−0.00012
Averaging in logit space instead of probability space increased log loss by 0.00012.
Logistic-regression stacking−0.00023
Jointly learning the blend weights and a recalibration slope increased log loss by 0.00023 compared with the fixed weighted average.
Hill-climbing ensemble selection−0.00007
Greedy selection with replacement over all four models. Landed within noise of the simple weight grid.
Feature-aware meta-model−0.00208
A LightGBM meta-model used base predictions and customer features to choose among models for each customer. It increased log loss by 0.00208, providing no evidence that model preference could be learned at the customer level.
Isotonic recalibration−0.00330
The initial evaluation fitted and scored the calibrator on the same rows, causing an apparent improvement. Under nested cross-validation, isotonic calibration produced the worst result in the ledger.
Platt scaling−0.00005
Two-parameter sigmoid fit. Essentially a no-op: the models are already well calibrated, confirmed independently by the reliability diagram.
Per-segment calibration−0.00030
Separate curves by credit-limit quartile and lateness count, since residuals vary sharply across those segments. The heterogeneity is real; correctable miscalibration inside it is not.
Base-rate shrinkage0.00000
This method moves probabilities towards the 22.12% base rate. The selected value, α = 1.0, applies no shrinkage and therefore provided no evidence that the original probabilities were overconfident.
Borrowed from the AMEX 1st/2nd/3rd place solutions — 5 tried, 0 transferred
Recency windows & within-customer ranks−0.00035
The test added three-month aggregates, contrasts between the recent and earlier three months, and within-customer ranks. Although the existing aggregates covered all six months, these shorter summaries increased log loss by 0.00035.
Very high min_data_in_leaf−0.00104
The AMEX reports used 211 rows per leaf to align cross-validation and leaderboard results. This represents 0.45% of their 458,913 rows, close to the existing 100 of 24,000 rows (0.42%) used here. Increasing the count therefore moved away from the comparable setting and steadily reduced performance.
DART boosting−0.00072
DART was the strongest model in the referenced AMEX solution. Its dropout method is better suited to a large tree ensemble; this dataset has 19 times fewer rows and supports fewer useful trees.
feature_fraction_bynode−0.00011
Per-split rather than per-tree feature sampling. Neutral-to-slightly-negative at both values tested.
Denoising & rank blendingn/a
These methods did not apply to this task. The AMEX transformation floor(x*100)/100 removes injected noise from floating-point columns, while this dataset already uses integers. Rank blending discards calibration and is unsuitable for log loss.
Structure — 3 splits tried, 0 helped
Segment models — by lateness−0.00225
Separate models for never-late / 1–2 / 3+ late months, where residual loss ranges 0.34 to 0.64. Each model early-stops on its own inner split, so small segments are not penalised for their size.
Segment models — by credit limit−0.00134
The two credit-limit groups were nearly equal in size (12,223 and 11,777), limiting any imbalance effect. Performance still declined, suggesting that both groups share the same feature-to-risk relationships and benefit from the larger combined training set.
Segment models — by card usage−0.00007
Never-used cards (1,716 customers) modelled apart from active ones. Exactly neutral.
Blend weight shrinkage−0.00002
The fitted 0.65/0.35 weights were moved halfway towards equal weights to reduce possible validation overfitting. Hidden-test log loss increased by 0.00013. The original weight surface was already stable from 0.50 to 0.70.
Data & variance — 4 tried, 2 worked
Repeated cross-validation+0.00093
Averaging predictions across six partitions reduced variance and improved OOF by 0.00093 in total. Most of the gain had been achieved after three partitions.
Full-data refit—
The refit improved hidden-test log loss by 0.00023 but cannot affect OOF, which contains no model trained on the full dataset. This illustrates a known limitation of the validation design.
Pseudo-labelling the test set−0.00024
Degraded monotonically as pseudo-row weight rose, under a leak-free protocol. No tuning direction to rescue it.

Completely flat. This LightGBM configuration is already stable across seeds — unlike the neural nets, where seed averaging matters a great deal.

Findings from the experiment record

Five recurring findings were identified across the 41 experiments.

New information contributed more than additional algorithms

Three of the ten model families received non-zero blend weights, while none of the seven later challengers were retained. Of the seven feature families tested, only spending decomposition produced a reliable improvement. It introduced a quantity absent from the raw columns. Supplying the same spending information to the GRU as an ordered channel changed log loss by only 0.00002, indicating that the information itself was more important than its representation.

7 challenger architectures → 0 kept
1 accounting identity → the largest gain of the project
re-presenting that same information in sequence form → +0.00002

Cross-validation measured feature and averaging gains differently

The spending features improved OOF log loss by 0.00046 and hidden-test log loss by 0.00173. By contrast, the sixth cross-validation partition improved OOF by 0.00024 but did not change the hidden-test score. Small OOF gains from additional averaging did not transfer to the hidden test, whereas the feature gain was larger on the hidden data.

features 3.8× amplified
averaging tweaks 0×

Low correlation alone did not improve the ensemble

Logistic regression had the lowest correlation with LightGBM at 0.948 but received zero weight. The GRU received 30% despite a higher correlation of 0.9825. A model’s errors therefore needed to be both different and useful.

logreg 0.948 corr → weight 0.00
GRU 0.9825 corr → weight 0.30

The available data does not explain some defaults

The twenty largest residuals were all defaulters assigned only 2–4% risk. Each had a clean payment history, moderate utilisation and a healthy credit limit. The factors associated with these defaults are not present in the supplied repayment, balance, payment or demographic variables.

worst 20 residuals: all clean histories, all defaulted

Methods from larger competitions did not transfer directly

Five techniques from the top three AMEX solutions were tested, but none improved performance. That competition contained 190 anonymised columns and 458,913 rows, supporting the generation and selection of thousands of aggregate features. This dataset has 23 interpretable columns and 24,000 rows; its largest gain came from using the column meanings to derive one new quantity.

AMEX 211 min-leaf = 0.45% of rows
this model 100 = 0.42%
equivalent proportions

Validation correction. Isotonic calibration initially appeared to improve performance because it was fitted and evaluated on the same out-of-fold predictions. Under nested cross-validation, it produced the worst result in the table. All subsequent fitted combiners and target-derived features were evaluated strictly out-of-fold.

Final model: 0.45 × LightGBM over 81 engineered features + 0.30 × bidirectional GRU over the six-month payment panel + 0.25 × TabPFN, combined as a plain weighted average. 270 cross-validated fold fits plus 11 full-data refits; probabilities used uncalibrated, verified against a reliability diagram. Out-of-fold log loss 0.42136, hidden test 0.40982.
