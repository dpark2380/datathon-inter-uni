Recovering the Number the Data Never States

The columns give statement balances and payments, never the amount actually charged. It is recoverable exactly, from the identity that governs a revolving account:

spend ₜ  =  BILL ₜ  −  BILL ₜ₊₁  +  PAY_AMT ₜ

The single largest move of the entire project, separating “spending more” from “stopped paying” when a balance rises.

04
Methodology & Experimentation

41 Experiments. 4 Survived.

A result worth reporting honestly

Isotonic calibration looked like the biggest win on record. It was fitted and scored on the same out-of-fold rows. Under proper nested cross-validation it became the single worst result in the table (−0.00330). Caught mid-project, and every fitted step after it was scored strictly out-of-fold.

Model Selection & Validation

Three Mechanisms, One Customer

One real validation customer: late the last two months, 100% credit utilisation, paid under 5% of a recent bill. Here is exactly how each component scores them.

LightGBM · gradient-boosted trees 62.7%

About 352 trees per fold, split on recency of lateness. One shown.

GRU · ordered 6-month memory 34.3%

Tells apart a customer who is recovering from one who is deteriorating.

TabPFN · pretrained, zero fitting 31.2%

Parameters never change; it never trains on our data.

0.45×62.7 + 0.30×34.3 + 0.25×31.2 = 46.3% predicted actual outcome: defaulted
